Who needs spells when you have relics?

For thousands of years, magic has been obtained through trades with the gods. Sacrifice magic materials upon their sacred altars, and the gods will offer magic relics in return. 

 But the world of magic and powerful relics has always been far out of reach for Dain, a simple trinket merchant, until masked cultists tried to sacrifice him to the gods. Vowing revenge, he decides to commit the ultimate taboo: make his own altar so he can obtain the power to hunt the cultists down.

Only, he accidentally creates an altar for a god who shouldn’t exist, and this god is a little different: it will only trade him cursed relics.

Dain will scour the world, hunt the magic beasts in his books, and barter their remains for greater and darker relics. His enemies and their ancient plots of calamity run deep. He’ll have to go even deeper to find them.

What to expect:

- LitRPG with gear-based progression

- Bartering magic relics with otherworldly gods

- Ancient schemes, vast worldbuilding, and epic battles 

- No harem

Cool but Overly Cliche

Reviewed at: Chapter 18 - Molkhara Golems
by Benw8888 Thursday, December 18, 2025 8:53:18 AM

This story has a cool premise/setting where you can sacrifice combinations of materials to eldritch gods to create relics, stat potions, and skills. However, the initial plotline and execution is very trope-y/cliche, which I find a shame given how the first chapter initially drew me in with the great worldbuilding.

Cliche moment examples:

1) Whenever there’s a threat, he somehow finds a way to make a new relic on the spot to combat the threat. Imagine a wolf outside your cart that’s howling and coming towards you, and you spend a couple minutes getting materials and talking with a god to make a relic.

2) He ‘coincidentally’ stumbles onto a ‘special’ person in need of saving in the forest who then joins his party and showers him with money. It’s both cliche and removes the subplot of him struggling to get money to get relic ingredients.

3) In general the MC is portrayed as hyper competent and surrounding characters are in awe of him despite it not feeling earned- he has no practical experience and has only read a book but is teaching someone like he’s a veteran adventurer, and a couple more of these examples.

I wish the MC was written as less experienced, with his cursed relics having stronger drawbacks, making it harder to craft relics, having him have less random cliche luck moments where everything goes his way. The background setting and overarching plot of cultists/traitors, his altar being forbidden, and himself potentially being hunted are cool, and I wish that was more of the focus.

This story would be much better if it was more grounded and down to earth.

Another Fantastic Start!

Reviewed at: Chapter 25 - The All-Seeing Eye
by SiriMythkiller Friday, November 28, 2025 2:17:58 AM

Overall: Another fantastic start for Maradina! Every aspect comes out swinging for the fences; another unique twist to the LitRPG system with compelling characters and intriguing story in a solidly built world. Possibly the strongest start to any of their stories!

Style: While gear does play an important part in almost all LitRPGs, none I have read have gear as the primary way to improve. And while things like stats and skills do matter, they don't seem to be something you can earn much on your own. All growth seems to come from relics, items that are gifted to mortals when proper offerings are presented to the deities (Curator Gods). The system is very light, without much to keep track of and without the genre standard blue boxes.

Worldbuilding: As always, Maradina has done a fantastic job weaving solid world building and system explanation throughout the story, pacing and placing it in just the right spot for readers while not making it feel awkward for the characters.

Story: The story comes out of the gate swinging with catastrophe and intrigue followed by a mad scramble for survival and stability that is still wavering precariously in these early chapters. It definitely feels like the bad guys have been working towards their goals for a long time and Dain (the main character) was just in the wrong place at the wrong time. Or rather, the right place, right time to become a big problem for the baddies down the line. So far, nothing feels forced for the sake of the plot (Maradina is excellent at avoiding this pitfall) with everything having logically sound explanations, even the oddities.

Characters: Dain (the main character) is a very realistically written protagonist. He often really has no options but bad ones and does his best just to make it through the current crisis. He's not overpowered at all; his greatest strengths are his quick and clever thinking backed by his theoretical knowledge he accumulated over years. He takes very reasonable risks in these early chapters, given his situation. The big thing I'm looking forward to character-development-wise is how Dain will go after the baddies once he's not focused on simply surviving, and how far he will be willing to go to get that vengeance.

Grammar: Maradina is one of the few authors I've read on Royal Road that will actively and quickly fix any grammar problems that slip in. These are usually just a tiny spelling mistake or a similar but not quite right word. Those that don't have nitpickey brains probably won't even notice. Due to Dain's...situation, there have been a few continuity errors where a plural was used instead of a singular but have already been fixed as of this review.

Fantastic characters, fun power system

Reviewed at: Chapter 70 - The Third Defiler
by TheHornedOne Friday, June 19, 2026 12:02:09 PM

 

I got to read the Alpha chapters when this was being written, and Im subscribed to the Patreon which is up to chapter 115 at the time of reviewing, and let me say the story starts good and keeps getting better. 

The world truly feels alive, and Dain’s love of relics and stories makes him the perfect vessel to explore it through. Instead of having some character around to explain stuff to him, he recognizes many of the creatures he sees and places he visits from stories and books, giving you a feeling that the world is old and explored, and you’re accompanying Dain as he explores it. The dialogue flows very naturally and the characters have great chemistry with each other, and their backgrounds let them each fill in parts of the world to paint an amazing picture for the reader.

 

The Relics are a really fun system to play with, and provide a very smooth and modular progression for all the characters, so you never know what a character might have up their sleeves. Their effects start off relatively simple (fire windball, produce light, etc.) but the farther the story gets the more powerful and intricate they can become. 

 

The cursed relics Dain uses are also a delight. The curses don’t feel niche to the point of not mattering, and they aren’t so crippling that he needs plot armor to survive them either. They add a feeling of unpredictability to the story because Dain as a certified relic nut knows almost everything about the more common relics, but cursed ones are a new thing to him. They make every fight feel tense because overuse of his more powerful relics could cripple him, but they also give him cards other people can’t predict.

One of the Best Stories Here

Reviewed at: Chapter 94 - Old Allies, New Allies
by UnboundMotion-17 Saturday, June 20, 2026 1:51:59 AM

I joined this story from the very beginning as a fan of Maradina’s other works, and this story is great. Very nonstandard power system as always, which is fun, MCs are the only ones actually safe from death in Maradina’s stories, and equipment feels like it’s getting the emphasis here that it never got in Blightburrow, so that’s always great. 

4.5 for style, everything’s great, but there’s nothing truly special about it to merit 5 stars. 

5 stars for story; I’m going to unironically compare the story to Brandon Sanderson — if I found out she was personally learning from him, I would be completely unsurprised. It’s both great, and Maradina has a similar habit of using a mix of tropes played straight and tropes turned on their head that makes Sanderson’s works so iconic, and Maradina pulls it off really well. End twist of book one heavily blindsided me, which is especially impressive with how many Fantasy stories I’ve consumed and how Autistic I am about them, and it both made a massive amount of sense looking back, and just more generally worked extremely well. 

5 stars for characters, and I wish I could give more. Every character, no matter who they are, feels like their own person. As someone for whom the characters are one of the most essential parts of a story for, that is incredibly impressive. Every once in a while, we get a chapter from a random dude’s perspective; I actively enjoy these chapters, because they’re rare, give us both a more grounded perspective on the story and information the MC wouldn’t be able to normally give us, and the throw away character feels like a person in their own right. There’s one chapter where we see through the eyes of some random kid who’d gotten drafted, and that’s exactly who it feels like we’re watching through. The jaded veteran deserter feels like that exactly, the perspective through the king’s eyes works really well; my problem with this section is that I have no way of actually articulating just how absurdly good the characters are. Recently, something came up that the MC knew about and the audience didn’t. Instead of some nonsense to change it so the MC wouldn’t understand or break character to explain it to the audience, instead there’s a different character present for whom not knowing about plot relevant thing is the only thing that makes sense, and if they did know it would be more character breaking, and the audience learns about the world not through the MC as is the easy answer, but through a different more ignorant character. This section deserves 6/5 stars if I were ruler of the universe

5 stars for grammar, any issues are quickly fixed on being pointed out, even those issues are really rare, and Maradina is really good at natural conversation and transitions. She’s also got a good vocabulary, so you don’t see the constant repetition born of a bad vocabulary, and she uses the best works to effectively do whatever she needs to do. The story also has great pacing, excellent balance of action and suspense, being allowed to breath and payoff. 

read this story, then go check out Maradina’s other stuff. She’s been growing as an author for a while now, this is like her 10th effective book including rewrites and a few stories she’s taken down and remade entirely, and I’ve yet to see a single book from her that was any worse than ‘just didn’t resonate with me’. This is definitely one that did resonate with me though, and I’m still avidly following, even as I’m struggling with College and burnout.
