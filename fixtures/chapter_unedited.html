Chapter 110 - Lightning Lodestones

After dinner, Cindralet folded itself into sleep. By ten, the hearthall had gone completely dark. The last cups had been washed, the last stools stacked away, and the last drowsy children guided home by elders. Even the many chickens, cows, and goats had quieted inside the barns, while the sparrows tucked into their nests on the rafters, ready to pass the night.

Dain, unfortunately, had no intention of sleeping yet.

He sat cross-legged on the roof of their barn beneath a cold, star-pricked sky, four wooden boxes of crystallized lightning arranged beside him. Rosary was there as well, and so was his owl construct perched atop her hat. That seemed to be its new favorite perching spot, only second to his own head. He’d tell them both to go away, but… he didn’t mind the company, he supposed.

As he ran his fingers through the boxes of crystallized lightning, he lifted a few shards, turned them, weighed them, and nodded absentmindedly to himself. He’d been doing this for a while now, checking the quality of the shards by measuring how clean and heavy they were—they were very clean and only moderately light—but every time he moved a shard, Rosary’s head tilted a fraction, evidently confused by his actions.

“So… are you petting the lightning rocks because they’re sad, or because you’re just weird?” she finally said. “Why’d you ask for them anyways? What’cha gonna do with them?”

It was easier to show her than to explain to her, so after he checked his last box and confirmed there weren’t any shards not up to par, he wiped his hands on a cloth and clapped.

The air in front of him twisted open with a reddish-purple swirl, and Belara’s portal unfolded quickly, its glow staining the wood, the boxes, Rosary’s beads, and the owl’s pale face. At this point, Rosary was completely used to seeing it, so she didn’t give any reaction whatsoever.

Belara’s hands did, though. The four pale hands slithered out, long-fingered and graceful, but just as Dain was about to lift a hand in greeting and bow his head, they started ‘looking’ around.

He held his tongue for a moment.

One pale hand tilted towards the east. Another curled downwards, fingertips brushing nothing. The other two trembled lightly while they swerved around, as if the mana in the wind itself had texture, and they could just… feel it.

“... You can tell we’re in a different country now, can’t you?” he eventually said.

But the hands kept ‘looking’ around, ignoring him so Rosary leaned in closer, whispering, “Can she really?”

“I think so,” Dain whispered back. “Her sensitivity to mana should be way sharper than mine, and Auraline’s called the Kingdom of Storms for a reason. To her, the mana in the air should feel a lot different from Obric’s.”

“Hm,” Rosary mumbled. “I guess she is a god after all.”

Then two of Belara’s hands turned palm-up, facing him. He straightened immediately. Belara was ready to do business now.

“Right,” Dain said, and immediately pushed the four boxes of crystallized lightning forward. “Hey, Belara. Relatively long time no see. Do you know what these things are?”

Belara’s hands ‘stared’ at him. He took that as a ‘yes, but not in terms of what purpose they serve in you cold-calling me’.

He reached into one box and lifted a shard between two fingers, showing it to her. “Today, we were helping the villagers bury these into the soil. The locals use them as fertilizer because lightning strikes the mountains of Peninsula Auraline so often and so violently that they crystallize at the peaks. Once they condense into shards, they can be safely harvested and shipped all across the world. Why, you ask? Because when the shards get buried, the mana stored inside disperses into the soil over time, enriching fields that shouldn’t be nearly this arable on the Brastel Continent. It’s why you’re seeing wheat fields all the way to the horizon even in the dead of winter. With crystallized lightning providing proper nutrients and warmth to the soil, any crop can go any time at all.”

Rosary tilted her head. So did his owl, and so did Belara’s hands, not getting his point. Dain sighed and picked up a second shard from the box with his other hand.

“However, when I was burying these shards today, I noticed this.”

He held the two shards close to each other. Nothing happened at first. He rotated the left one, then the right one, then pulled them apart. Still nothing. Refusing the urge to clench his jaw, he brought them closer once more at different angles and adjusted their orientations over and over again, staring intently at the shards.

Suddenly, the two shards snapped together with a sharp clang. Rosary jerked slightly and his owl fluffed itself on her hat. The golden light trapped inside both shards flashed almost pure white for the briefest instant before settling back into its usual glow, but now it was slightly brighter.

Dain grinned. He rotated one of them out of alignment, then pried them apart—though even that took more strength then it should’ve.

“... I knew it,” he breathed, looking at Belara’s hands. “These shards are natural lodestones with magnetic properties that allow them to attract or repel each other. I already had an inkling of a guess that they had this property—back in Corvalenne, sometimes lightning would strike so hard that a few nuts and bolts in Sorowyn Carpentry would lift off the counters for the briefest of moments—but it wasn’t until just now that I, too, learned that shards of crystallized lightning retain the magnetic properties… and this is important, because while there are many, many different types of lodestones in the world, all of them have one thing in common.” He took a peek at Rosary, smiling. “Wanna guess?”

“Uhh…” Rosary started running her hands, checking the hundreds of ribbons wrapped around her body for a clue. “They’re… not that tasty?”

“Well, I don’t know about that,” he muttered, turning back to face Belara, “but all lodestones can be used as side offerings to fuse multiple relics into just one relic. Their magnetic properties lend them the ability… or so it seems to from the perspective of the gods. I can’t claim to understand how the gods upgrade mortalkind’s relics, but…”

With his sales pitch completed, he began stripping.

The first to go was his wingcloak, but his four silverplume wings stirred, and they folded around his shoulders, then around his arms, hugging him and refusing to let go. He had to scold them and pry them off his arms one by one before setting the whole wingcloak atop the leftmost box of shards. Then, beside the wingcloak, he placed his void archivist’s satchel as well as his entire set of frostnerve clothes, which he hadn’t been wearing in the first place.

He hesitated a little while taking off his satchel, but a merchant who clung to his stock forever was a hoarder, not a merchant.

“For the first relic fusion, I’d like you to bind these my wingcloak, my frostnever clothes, and my satchel using one whole box of crystallized lightning,” he said to Belara, patting the box in its entirety. “Going off their purity, their weight, and their generally smooth texture, I’d say these shards are all about Uncommon-0 grade across the box. They should suffice as binding materials for fusing these relics together.”

Then he moved on. Next to go was his brooch of a limbo star from his collar and his owlbone whistle hanging from his neck, both of which he placed atop the second box of shards from the left.

“For the second fusion, I’d like you to bind my brooch and my whistle using another box. After this…”

He took off his owl mask also hanging around his neck, popped out his prosthetic left eye—which took a bit of courage, squeezing his fingers past his eyelids—and then reached for his owl construct perched on Rosary’s hat. His owl screeched at him, refusing to go anywhere, so he had to grab it with both hands and stroke its feathers until it calmed down, finally allowing him to place it atop the third box from the left.

“For the third fusion, I’d like you to bind my owl construct, my mask, and my fearlight eye into one relic. I don’t like letting things go to waste, though, so for the final box…”

He placed his cane, his wraith rage perfume, and his spiritbane branch—which he’d taken out of his satchel before this—atop the rightmost box.

“Fuse these three into one,” he finished, looking at Belara expectantly. “Is it possible?”

The longest deliberation he’d ever seen Belara take followed.

Dain had made offerings to her enough times to start to recognize her moods by hand movement alone. Sometimes her hands were swift, almost eager, but sometimes her hands moved slowly, almost teasingly. Sometimes they looked a little judgemental, and sometimes they looked perplexed about what to do with his offerings. This time, she was definitely a little hesitant.

He didn’t know what she was hesitant about. As he’d said, he couldn’t claim to know what sort of mechanism bound the gods’ actions and abilities, so all he could do was look at her sincerely… and maybe with a bit of childlike excitement as well.

Fusing relics was something he’d always wanted to try doing as a kid and as a fledgeling merchant.

Come on, Belara.

You can do this, right?

You’re not… unable, right?

And—as if in response to his quiet doubt—Belara finally moved after an entire minute of silence, each hand seizing a box and yanking them into the portal.

Dain sighed a breath of relief, then sat back with his arms propped behind him as he waited for her to return.

“... What are relic fusions, anyways?” Rosary mumbled, still feeling the ribbons wrapped around her arms, torso, and legs. “Maybe ‘old’ me wrote something about it. Relic fusions, relic fusions… no, this one says not to trust blue soup. This one says refer to my right knee for topics related to relics, and this one… no, wait. That’s my left knee—”

“Relic fusion is when you use lodestones as side offerings to bind multiple relics into one relic,” Dain said, keeping his eyes on the portal. “Typically, the fused relic ends up stronger than the sum of its parts because the lodestones are also magic materials—so you’re adding to the overall value of the relics—but another big reason why people fuse relics is to cut down on the total number of relics they have to carry.”

“‘Cause… carrying many relics is heavy?”

“Partly. But weight’s the least interesting problem.” Dain glanced aside, checking if there were any villagers looking up at them. “For the longest time, I’ve been running around with eight or nine relics on me, which means there’s a lot for me to think about in fights… but Borik and Alvara didn’t have eight or nine relics. They only have four to five relics, which means they don’t have to split as much focus between them, and they don’t have to worry so much about how much mana to split between their relics.”

“Ah.” Rosary pulled her chin back. “That makes sense… I think?”

“Sure, fused relics typically have higher mana costs to compensate for the fact that they’re typically stronger relics as well, but the overall mana cost should still be lower than the sum of all the component relics,” he finished. “If I can trim my relics down, I’d be able to distribute my mana across them more efficiently. It’ll also take me less time to re-equip them if I ever have to take them off for a disguise. Remember how long it took us to re-equip all our relics back when we were running through Fortress Mavrekir? We wouldn’t have to deal with that if we had less relics overall.”

“Ah… Ah!” Rosary grinned now, taking off her hat and twirling it on her fingertip. “So can I do it too? Can I fuse my ribbons and hat into one relic and make them stronger?”

“They’re Cognitum-Class relics, right?” He thought for a second. “Normally, you can only ask for relic fusions if the component relics are the same class, but my relics are a little different. If you could find an Altar to Utnar, you can get it done there.”

“Why can’t I just use your portal now?”

“You want to convert perfectly fine relics into cursed relics that’ll debilitate you?”

Rosary paused at that.

“... Actually, nevermind,” she muttered. “But I am still taking some shards of crystallized lightning with me when we leave.”

“Me too. Lodestones will always come in handy.”

But before either of them could scramble down to sneak out a few more boxes of crystallized lightning, the first of Belara’s pale hands returned.

This time, with his fused relics.
